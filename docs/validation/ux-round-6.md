# UX round 6 — the date on a row is a control (issue #107)

Sixth on-screen review. The finding, in the owner's words: *"when I see a date like yesterday or tomorrow I would like to be able to click on that and the date picker pops up. Most of the time what I want to do is re-plan something, so it's much easier to have that as a first click instead of opening the card and then clicking again on the date."* Re-planning is the most frequent thing done while reading a list and it cost three interactions — open the card, find the Due field, click the calendar. Now it costs one.

Two constraints came with it: **don't change how the chip looks** (only widen what you can hit, especially on the phone), and **the date, the folder and the AI conversation must each have their own space** — stacking one below the others rather than letting their tap areas fight. Judged on the seeded disposable instance (synthetic data only) at 1440×900 and 390×844, light and dark; every screenshot below was read back before the round was called done.

## What this round did not do

The date's tap area stops at **35 px tall**, not the fleet spec's 44. It cannot reach 44 without either taking the 6 px above it — which belongs to `.trow-main`, the row-open target, so tapping the bottom of a title would open the picker (a regression the walk caught and rejected) — or deepening the card, which adds 9 px to every phone row carrying a date and pushes the dense rows past round 2's ≤96 px budget. Two of this app's own contracts, one row: the density budget won, because it is the one chosen for this surface deliberately. `tests/e2e/test_story_07_phone.py` asserts the 32 px floor the chip does meet, so a later shrink still fails loud; raising the row budget is the prerequisite for ever making this a 44 px target.

Three things measured on the pre-change build and left alone, because they are older than this round and none of them got worse: the folder and AI glyphs' 13 px upward expansion overlaps `.trow-main` on 36 rows across the sweep; one row's date and AI glyph overlap at 320 px wide on the Tree; and four rows already exceed the ≤96 px budget at 320 px. The sweep returns **the same 37 overlaps and the same 4 over-budget rows before and after** this change.

## Side effect worth recording

`today.js` recovered a row's ISO date by **parsing the due chip's `title` attribute** to decide the overdue tint. Widening that tooltip to say the chip is clickable silently cost Today its overdue colouring — caught by story 05, not by reading the code. The tooltip is no longer load-bearing: the chip carries `data-due` and `today.js` reads the value as a value. The tooltip itself went back to the bare ISO date it always showed.

`dueInput()` — the text-box-plus-picker pair — had exactly one caller, the Table's cell, and is deleted with it. `duePicker()` stays the one owner of the coarse-pointer branch, now with four callers.

## Verification

- Screenshots above: captured in a headed Chromium (desktop) and headed WebKit (phone) walk on the seeded disposable instance, read back one by one; no page errors in either leg. The walk drove the real thing — a click on the chip calls `showPicker()` (recorded via a page-level stub, since a native calendar is an OS widget no automation can see), the picked date PATCHes, and the row re-renders on it.
- `scripts\verify-before-ship.ps1` green: byte-compile · ruff · **323 unit** · e2e full tier, **14 tests**. Story 04 now walks the Table cell opening the picker instead of typing into a box; story 07 gains the three-target geometry — built on a deliberately bare row (date + folder + AI and nothing after them) so all three land side by side, asserting no shared pixels, the 44 px floor on the two glyphs, the date's own 32 px floor, containment inside the card and the ≤96 px budget. Proven to fail without the fix: dropping the separation margin makes `assert_no_overlap` fail on that row.
- Not verified here: the same walk on the real phone over the tailnet (owner-only — the standing item in [validation.md](../validation.md)'s owner checklist).
