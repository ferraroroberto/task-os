# Views

The exhaustive index of every surface — each pane, menu, sheet, dialog, gesture and key, how it is reached and why it is shaped as it is — is the [UX surface map](ux-surface-map.md); this page holds the behaviour behind the main views.

PC-first: Board and Today are two-dimensional (lanes, a calendar lane) and span the window, declared in `.fleet.toml` (`[design] wide_views`), while Time, History, Search and Settings are one-dimensional and keep the centred 772px measure (design.md Layout) — the Settings page holds it on its own, and the Email archiving report, a nine-column table, opens in a sheet that spans the window; the phone gets the same views as an adaptive second rendering (bottom pill, the archiving report as cards, the task drawer full-screen). The nav holds five destinations (Today · Board · Time · History · Search), the pill's ceiling; Settings is the gear in the header, one tap from every tab, and the batch-archiving report is its *Email archiving* row. The Table view — a grid and a Tree outliner — is gone; a stored `table` / `tree` tab or an old `#table` link lands on Today.

- **Today leads, and is where the app opens** — the nav reads **Today · Board · Time · History · Search** (Settings is the header gear; Time, see [Time tracking](time-tracking.md#time-tracking)), on the phone's bottom pill and the desktop rail alike, and a first visit lands on Today on every pointer. A deep link (`#task/<id>`, `#search`, `#journal`, `#settings/…`) still wins over the landing tab, and the last tab you left is remembered per browser (`task-os.tab`), so only a fresh browser opens on Today.
- **One row, one filter state, every view** — Board, Today and the Search tab's task hits are *renderings* of the same filtered list, drawn with the same row (`rows.js`), the fleet's vendored **action-row**: a leading **completion circle** on a mouse or trackpad (complete an open task — a recurring one rolls to its next date — or reopen a closed one, with Undo; a touch screen has no circle: a task closes by swiping it right, or by opening it and setting its status, and the keyboard's `e` works everywhere), the one-line ellipsized title over **one quiet meta line on a budget** (the vendored split meta) — **one** context name first, the only part the ellipsis may take: the project when the row does not lead with it as a pill (Today and the Board lead the title with the project as a pill instead, so its meta line names none), else the issue code; then, whole at the right edge behind a drawn dot, the figures: due (relative, with the repeat glyph when it recurs; overdue and due today in the attention tone) · **exception chips only**, neutral, in the one status-chip tone map: *Blocked* or the day its start arrives (clock glyph), an *Inbox* / *Standby* status where the view does not already say it, and *High* (no mark for medium or low, no danger tone) · at most two counts, children and comments. No figure is ever cut by the ellipsis. The folder, AI conversation and issue links and the person are not on the row: the ⋯ menu opens each link and the drawer shows them all. On a mouse or trackpad every open row outside the Board's lanes also shows **Move** — the one visible verb (a calendar glyph on a 44px icon button between the title and the kebab, unpainted like the kebab), because re-dating is most of what gets done to a task: one click opens the date sheet, one pick moves it. Then one trailing **⋯ kebab** holding every other action (Move…, status, priority, open folder / AI conversation / issue, details; also right-click, `.` or the menu key on a focused row). **On a touch screen a row swipes** (`swipe.js`): right completes it (with Undo), left opens the date sheet to move it — never the only way to do either, and never in the phone Board's Status carousel, whose columns swipe sideways already; on the phone each list is an inset rounded card at the page gutter, and the swipe reveal is clipped to its corners. Nothing on the meta line is a target, so a tap anywhere on the row opens it; 60px a row. Hairlines between rows; on the phone the rows sit inside an inset card. Above every view sits a **top strip** on one line — the live **text filter**, always on screen because it is the one reached for most, with the **filter button** beside it, then the quick-add `+` (and, on a wide screen, *Select*; on a phone Select is a row of the filter sheet). The list's count is the text field's placeholder (*Filter 12 tasks…*), its one home; the filter button carries how many filters are on (and its name spells them out — *Filters, 2 on: todo · Home renovation*) and opens the **Filters & sort** sheet (`filters.js`, the vendored editor modal): **sort** first, one segmented control of five peers (Due · Priority · Modified · Created · Title; the strip's sentence says *last modified*), then, on Today and the Board, the view toggles (the Board's **Layout** and **AI triage**, **Kind of task**, **Whose tasks**), then **status (ticks, several allowed, incl. `done` / `cancelled`, so finished items are findable)**, project, **person (ticks, several allowed)**, due window and modified window (incl. its inverse — **untouched > 30/60/90 days**, the weekly stale pass: do it, defer it with a start date, or admit it's dead; any write counts as a touch, sync and mirror included, so a GitHub-synced task never looks stale), applied as they change (on Search the status row is drawn with the open statuses ticked, and un-ticking them all searches every status, done and cancelled included; on Today and Board it is drawn with Todo ticked, and the ticks are also the **status pills** inside the text field — see below), with *Clear* beside *Done* while something is on; on the phone the ticks sit two per line. The state **is the URL** — `?status=todo&project=12&sort=updated&updated=week` is the same shareable view on every tab; the default is open tasks (plus today's done tasks for the Board's last column); on Today and Board the status filter is shown as removable **pills inside the text field** — the fleet chip with a ×, one line, scrolling sideways rather than wrapping — and the pills and the sheet's status ticks are one state: it rests on a *Todo* pill, so a typed search covers to-do tasks only, never finished work crowding it; × on the last pill (or un-ticking every status in the sheet) searches every status, × on one of several drops just that one, and ticking statuses in the sheet draws them as pills. The pill is the scope of the search: with no text the list is still the open tasks (the Board keeps its Inbox and Standby columns), a picked status narrows the list as before, and the header line names what a query covers (*todo*, the picked statuses, *every status*). Today and the Board carry a **Kind of task** row of the filter sheet and Search a switch under its box, both **Tasks · Issues · All** (the vendored segmented control): **Tasks** (the default) leaves out the issues the forge sync brings in, **Issues** shows only those (a task carrying an issue ref), **All** both; the pick is the URL's `?scope=` (absent for Tasks), so it survives a reload; on Today and the Board it counts toward the filter button and the sheet's *Clear* resets it, on Search *Clear* leaves it alone. **Whose tasks.** With two or more members (`people` in the config), Today and the Board carry the **person row** (Mine · each member · Everyone, described under the Board) as the sheet's **Whose tasks** row, under Kind of task: **Mine** (the default) is the tasks whose owner is the person this device is signed in as — each phone signs in with its own token, so it opens on its own tasks with no picker step; plain loopback on this PC is the install owner, and a task nobody gave an owner counts as the install owner's — **Everyone** every member's. Mine and Everyone are the device's: remembered under the `task-os.whose` key (Mine when the store is unreadable) and mirrored into the URL as `?whose=everyone` (absent = Mine), so a shared link says what it shows; an explicit `?whose=` beats the stored pick, and reading a link never rewrites it. Picked people are the URL's `?owner=` and win over both. The row is the other axis from the scope, not part of it; off its default (Everyone, or picked people) it counts toward the filter button and the sheet's *Clear* resets it; the journal follows it. **Search always covers everyone** — the Search tab's task hits ignore it (the other filters still apply), it shows no person row, and the command palette searches everyone too. With one member there is no row and nothing changes but the scope's label. The narrowing is the list API's own `?owner=` filter, so the counts and the journal's older-weeks probe agree with what is listed. Today's horizons sit under quiet uppercase section headers with their counts, and inside each horizon the rows are one flat list in the Board's order (due day, then no project, then project A–Z, then title), the project the row's leading pill and the assignee pill beside it in the Everyone view — no project sub-headers. On a wide screen (≥ 1024 px, beside the detail pane) the list scrolls inside its own box under the header and text filter, as a Board lane does, so a long list never puts every row's controls on screen at once; the phone scrolls the page. **Move** (the row's verb, its swipe left, the menu's *Move…*, the `d` key and Select mode's Move) opens the date sheet: one 4 × 2 grid of chips, *Today · Tomorrow · This Fri · Weekend · Next week · Next Mon · Month end · Pick…*, each over the date it lands on (`GET /api/dates`), the task's own day tinted, then *No date* under the grid; the drawer's Due block is the same grid. A status beside a title (a child, a blocker, a palette hit) is a chip only when it is an exception — an Inbox arrival (accent), standby (neutral) — in the one status-chip tone map; todo and done are a quiet word.
- **Board** — a **week planner**, with the status columns behind a segment. The Filters & sort sheet holds **Week · Status** as its **Layout** row (the vendored segmented control; the pick is remembered on the device, `task-os.board.mode`, and, like the theme, is neither counted on the filter button nor cleared), then **Tasks · Issues · All** and the **person row** (two or more members); nothing sits between the top strip and the lanes. **Week**, the default, lays the open tasks out by due date: **Today** (overdue included) · **Tomorrow** · **This week** · **This weekend** · **Next week** · **Later**, the tasks with no date closing *Later* under a *No date* sub-heading. A week runs Monday to Sunday (on a Sunday, "this week" is the one tomorrow starts), and *This week* and *This weekend* are there only while they have days left after tomorrow, so every date has exactly one lane. Inside a lane the rows run by due day, then — within one day — the tasks with no project first and the projects A–Z, then title A–Z; a sort picked in the filter sheet replaces that order. Each row leads its title with its project as a pill (the vendored chip; a lane under 280px wide, as the six Week lanes of a desktop window are, shows it at the start of the meta line's context slot instead, so the title keeps its width) and, whenever more than one person is on the row (**Everyone**, or two or more picked), a second pill on the meta line naming who the task is assigned to — one person alone shows none, because every row is theirs. **The person row** is the same vendored segmented control as Tasks · Issues · All: **Mine** first, each other member, then **Everyone** last, the raised segment the state. A tap picks that one person (the row is a single choice); a long name clips with an ellipsis and its full text is the segment's tooltip. The control holds at most five segments, so with more than three members besides you the row keeps the picked and the recently picked ones and folds the rest behind **+N more**, a sheet with a name filter and one tick per person. The sheet is where several people can be picked at once (Mine and a person together show both, a set that empties falls back to Mine); **+N more** is the raised segment while the pick is several people. The picks live in the URL as `owner=<member id>,…` (`person=` stays the contact filter, so the two combine), Mine and Everyone as before (`whose=everyone`, remembered per device); Today shares the row and the keys, the Search tab ignores them. The project filter is the filter button's sheet, one tap from the strip. **Drag a row onto another lane** to re-date it to that lane's first day (the Move sheet's *No date* clears a date): it is the date sheet's own Move, with the same toast and Undo, and nothing but the due date changes. **Status** shows **Inbox · Todo · Standby · Done today** (done today = completed on the current local day; tick `done` in the filter sheet to see all done tasks instead); drag a row onto another column to change its status, and status ticks hide the columns not picked. In both modes an empty lane collapses: to its header on a wide screen, where it is still a drop target, and out of the way on the phone. On a wide screen (≥ 1024 px) the lanes sit side by side using the full width, each scrolling on its own under a sticky header with its count; the six week lanes share the window and never scroll it sideways, a long title shortening with an ellipsis instead. On the phone Week is one list of lane sections (the rows swipe there) and Status a one-column scroll-snap carousel whose count strip doubles as the column switcher. Flat regions split by 1px hairlines. The page header names the Board's exceptions: Inbox arrivals in the accent, then what is overdue or due today (*4 in Inbox · 3 overdue*).
- **Select several tasks at once** — the **Select tasks** row of the Filters & sort sheet (on a wide screen also the checklist toggle in the Board's and Today's top strip) turns every card/row into a tick target; on a phone the strip carries no Select until the mode is on, and then it reads **Done**, the way out while nothing is ticked yet (drag is off while it is on, and a tap on the phone selects rather than opening — the horizontal swipe still belongs to the Board's carousel). It is **one selection**: tick three cards on the Board, switch to Today, and the same three are ticked there. With at least one ticked, the bulk bar takes the top strip over, on **one line** with every control at the strip's own square height — *N selected* · **Move** first, as on the row (the one date sheet — the push-out phrases with their dates, *Pick a date…*, *No date* — so a pick moves the whole selection) · **set status** (`complete` included, so a recurring task in the selection rolls its due instead of closing, exactly as its own row select would) · **Set owner…** (only with two or more members: a person-glyph square whose pick hands the whole selection — a project's children, say — to that member) · ✕ to leave. On a phone with the owner square showing, the count drops the word *selected* so *Set status…* stays readable. One `POST /api/tasks/bulk` per action, applied through the same repo layer as a single-task edit, so every task gets its own activity row. A task that fails — deleted in another tab, say — is **named in the toast** (`1 updated · 1 failed (#36: …)`), never dropped in silence; Escape leaves Select mode. The bar's fourth square is **delete**: one confirmation names the count (and how many are projects whose children go too, and how many are synced coding tasks the next sync would bring back), then `POST /api/tasks/bulk/delete` with the same per-id report.
- **One key per action** — with a row focused, `e` completes, `1`–`3` set the status, `t` / `w` / `d` move the due date, `p` cycles priority, `z` undoes, `?` lists them; with tasks ticked the same key acts on the whole selection. See [Keyboard triage](task-workflow.md#keyboard-triage).
- **History** — what I changed, newest first: my own activity (every field a write changed, old → new) and my comments, one overline per local day and one row per task for each run of consecutive changes to it. The row's meta line is the newest change and its time; a row with more than one change has a chevron that lists them all under it, and the row opens the task's drawer. Kind chips (**All · Date moved · Closed/reopened · Status · Priority · Moved · Comments**, each with its count) re-read the feed narrowed to one kind and are remembered on the device (`task-os.history.kind`); **All** also shows the edits no chip names (title, notes, folder …). It opens on today and **Show the day before** widens it a day at a time, up to 31. *Own* means signed with the name this request writes as (plus the unnamed default `me` for the install owner), so the mirror, the AI and the issue sync, which write as themselves, are left out. An empty day and a feed that cannot be read are their own states; the feed is `GET /api/history` over `src/tasks/history.py`. Not recorded, so not listed: link edits, comment edits and deletes, and anything on a task that was since deleted (its activity goes with it).
- **Done journal** — everything closed, newest day first, one flat heading per local calendar day (`Yesterday · Tue 1 Sep · 2 done`) over the same rows (the filled circle reopens a task in place); cancelled tasks ride along muted, with a switch in the head to drop them. A reading surface, not a tab: open it from the command palette (*Journal*) or the **journal** link in the Board's Done column head — `#journal` deep-links it (`#journal/task/42` with the drawer open), the shared filter sheet applies and rides the URL (project · person · text; status, due, modified and sort are hidden because they say nothing about a closed task), and any tab press leaves. The page is this week; **Show the week before** widens it seven days at a time, and the journal only says it has reached the end once the server confirms nothing closed earlier. Friday afternoon it answers "what actually happened this week"; the weekly review will quote it. `done_at` is the **closed-at** stamp — set when a task enters `done` *or* `cancelled`, cleared when it reopens (schema v10 backfills cancelled tasks from their activity log) — and the day is the local calendar day, the same rule as the Board's *Done today*. Terminal: `tasks journal [--weeks N]`.
- **Today** — every open task, in four horizons nearest first (with the Table gone, this is where any open task is found, by the text filter, which works across all four, or by scrolling, every horizon being open): what is **due** — ≤ today, overdue first, then today — then **Soon** (tomorrow … +7 days), **Later** (further out) and **No date**, each one flat list sorted like a Board lane (due day first, then no project, project A–Z, title — so an earlier date sits above a later one whatever its project, and a schedule change always reorders it), the same rows (status changed through the select — a recurring task also gets a **complete** option that rolls its due one cadence forward instead of closing; **done** always closes for good, recurring or not); the project leads each row as a pill, as on the Board. The tab opens on what is due: those rows come first with no heading of their own, because the page header names them — *2 overdue · 3 due today* in the attention tone, or a muted *Nothing due today* — read off the very counts the list was drawn from, so the header and the list never disagree. The header no longer repeats an open count on any tab; the other views name their own exceptions there as the redesign reaches them. A row carries no date control of its own: its controls stay within the action-row budget (the circle, the Move verb, the ⋯ menu). **Move…** is the ⋯ menu's date entry (or the `D` key) — *Today · Tomorrow · This Fri · Weekend · Next week · Next Mon · Month end · Pick… · No date* — which moves the task's due date and leaves it on the list in its new place, with an Undo in the toast; the start date is the drawer's field alone (see [Start dates](task-workflow.md#start-dates)). **Soon**, **Later** and **No date** sit below under the Board's own group header — the quiet uppercase name with its count, no hairline and no chevron — and are always open, so the whole list reads without a tap and nothing is ever folded away from a text filter; Later and No date only show when they hold something. This is the landing tab on every pointer (the last tab is remembered after that). **On a desktop window (1024px and up) Today is a split view**: the list takes the left half and the right half is the task detail, so a row's ⋯ menu sits near its title rather than at the far edge of the window. The right half is never empty while a task is due: arriving on Today with nothing open shows the first due task there by itself, without writing a `#task/` link (a reload picks the first due task again), and the row keys keep working beside it; leaving Today closes it. Opening a task (a tap, Enter, or ⋯ → *Open details*) fills the half with the same drawer the other tabs use, `#task/<id>` still deep-links, and closing it reads *Select a task to see its details*. The Board keeps the 440px side panel, and the phone keeps the full-screen sheet.
- **Task drawer** — a right-hand side panel on desktop (≥ 1024 px, the list stays visible beside it), full-screen on the phone; deep-linkable as `#task/42`. It is ordered by the usual flow on a task — set its status, re-plan the due date, read and add a comment, close it — and every control comes from the design tokens: one type scale (the title in `heading-md`, section titles in `body` bold, reading text in `body`, labels in `label`), one field style and one button hierarchy, with **Send** the drawer's only primary button. Top to bottom: the **head** — the breadcrumb as one button (it opens the parent picker, below), an *Inbox* tag on a task still in Inbox, a **···** with *Open <parent>* and **Delete task** (last, after a divider; its header names the task's code), and close → the editable **title** (a field with a visible underline) → **Status** as a segmented control, **To do · Standby · Cancelled · Done**: the raised segment is the state, one tap moves it, and an Inbox task raises none (any segment files it). **Done closes the drawer** and the toast offers **Undo**; a recurring task's Done rolls it to its next date. → **Due** as the date chips — the same 4 × 2 grid the row's date sheet shows, each chip a phrase over the day it lands on (`GET /api/dates`), the task's own day tinted, *Pick…* the calendar → **Comments**, newest first: the latest three, then *Show N older*. One compact row each: a narrow time column (the time today, *Yest.*, then a short date; the full stamp and the origin in its tooltip), the text clamped to two lines (a tap on the row opens the rest) and, only on somebody else's comment, their name after the text in muted type. A comment holding exactly one web link *is* that link: the text around the URL opens it in a new window, with a small open-in-new mark, and the raw URL is not shown; a comment that is only a URL, holds two or more targets, or links an AI conversation, a folder or an issue ref keeps its chips (`{folder}` refs and `repo#N` as clickable chips). The comment field heads the list on a desktop and is **pinned to the foot of the sheet on the phone**, so it is always one tap away (Ctrl+Enter sends, `origin = ui`). One **···** per row opens **Edit** (the body in place: Ctrl+Enter saves, Escape cancels; the author, time and origin are untouched, an edit corrects the text and never re-attributes it) and **Delete**, which asks first, since there is no undo → the **property list**, one inset group of 44 px rows: **Links** (the folder, the linked issue and every link as pills that open on one click — an AI-conversation URL wears the bot chip, see [AI conversation links](ai-conversation-links.md#ai-conversation-links) — and **+**, which unfolds the folder editor and the link editor under the group), **Due** typed in place (*fri*, *in 2 weeks*, an ISO date; × clears it), **Owner** (with two or more members — see below), **Person**, **Parent**, **Repeat** (with its *Every N* and fixed-day rows once there is a cadence) and, last and smaller, **Priority**. Owner, Person, Repeat and Priority each open the row menu, so a change is two taps; Parent opens the parent picker → **Notes** (the task's markdown description): a tap on the text edits it in place, leaving the field saves it, Escape cancels → **More fields**, a disclosure closed by default whose summary names what is set inside (*starts in 3d · CODE-1 · 1 blocker*); once opened it stays open for the page's life, and a reload closes it again. Under it: starts, code, blocked by, children (click to navigate, add child) and the **issue panel**: the linked issue (provider glyph, `repo#N` chip, state, labels, last synced, *Sync now*, *Unlink*) or, for a plain task, *Create issue* (repo from the last-seen list or typed) and *Link existing* (`owner/repo#N` or the URL). See [Issues as tasks](issues-as-tasks.md#issues-as-tasks). → **Activity**, folded behind its count (*6 changes*): `field old → new · actor · time`, where the issue sync's rows fold behind one more *Show N sync updates* toggle so your own changes lead. **Delete task** (in the head's ···) asks first: a confirmation names the task and, when it has one, its subtree (*Its 3 child tasks go with it*); confirm and the task is gone with its comments, links and history (no trash, no undo — the dialog says so), the drawer closes, every view refreshes, a toast says how much went. A **synced coding task whose issue is still open** gets a warning in the same dialog: the next issue sync recreates it while the issue stays open, so unlink it first or close the issue — the delete is still yours to confirm, but never a silent resurrection ten seconds later. A drawer at least 720 px wide (Today's half of a large screen) lays the content (status, due, comments, notes, more, activity) beside the property list; narrower, it is the one column above. **With a task open the keys act on it:** `1` `2` `3` set Inbox, To do and Standby, `X` is Done (and closes), `T` and `W` move the due date to tomorrow and next week, `D` opens the calendar, `P` cycles the priority, `C` puts the cursor in the comment field, `E` opens the folder and `L` unfolds the folder and link editors; `Z` undoes and `Esc` closes. On a mouse or trackpad each control shows its key, and a line under the property list lists them.

  **Changing or removing a parent.** The breadcrumb in the head (accessible name *Change parent of <title>*) and the **Parent** row both open the picker sheet — a centred card on a desktop, the whole screen on a phone — with a filter field whose placeholder counts the tasks (*Filter 44 open tasks*), then **Recent** (the parents this device filed tasks under last) and **No parent (top level)**, then every **open** task in tree order, indented by depth, each with its path as muted text; the task itself, its descendants and closed tasks are not offered, and any open task can become a parent (it turns into a project the moment it has a child, and stops being one when the last child leaves). Recent hides while a filter is typed. A tap, or Enter on the filter's first match, files the task there through `POST /api/tasks/{id}/move`; *No parent* sends `parent_id: null`, with no confirmation and no Undo. The server stays the authority — a target that became a descendant while the picker was open is refused with a `cycle` toast. This is the one way to re-parent from the UI.

  **Whose task it is.** Every task has an **owner** — a member of the install (`people` in the config), not the drawer's *Person* under More fields, which stays the contact a task is *about*. With two or more members the drawer's head carries an **Owner** select beside status and priority, on the desktop panel and in the phone's full-screen drawer: pick another member and the task is theirs at once, with an `owner old → new` row in the activity log (by name). A new task is owned by whoever made it — the member whose own token signed the request, or the install owner at this PC — and a subtask by its creator, not its parent. A task nobody ever gave an owner (everything from before schema v20, a synced issue, an email capture) is the install owner's. With one member there is no control at all.
- **Settings** (the header gear) — the fleet's full Settings page (the vendored `settings-group`): a modal dialog over the current tab, full screen on a phone and a centred 772px column on desktop, its title and × in a sticky header; × or Escape returns to the tab, which stays as it was under it. Six inset groups with sentence-case labels: **This device** (Text size, changed in place · Full screen on Edge, in Edge only · Row actions · Command palette · Phone access), **Sync** (Issues as tasks · Calendar), **Email** (Capture into Inbox · Email archiving), **Tools** (Time categories · Search · Folder opener · Voice quick-add · AI Inbox triage) and **Available features** (one switch per feature block, see [Setup and migration](setup-and-migration.md)) and **This install** (Your name · Database · Mirror and backup). Each row shows its current value — *on* · *synced* · *off* · *3 filed* …, in the attention or danger tone when it is an exception — and its chevron opens the row's **sheet**, which holds the card that row replaced, every control and reading unchanged. The sheet is the vendored modal over the page on every width, its title and × pinned at the top and **Done** pinned at the bottom (the Email archiving report's sheet spans the window). `#settings/<row>` keeps a sheet open across a reload, and Back closes it. A line under the page's header names what needs you: the mails the last archiving run left (*3 mails need you*), then a failing service (*Issue sync failing*, *Backup failing*), else a plain *6 of 9 services on*.
- **Settings → Email archiving** — the one surface that is not a rendering of the task list: the sheet carries the *Archive Inbox now* button, a **first N mails** bound (empty = the whole Inbox), the archiver's own state (configured / running / the model it ranks with / the last run / *not configured* with the reason) and what the runs on the picker add up to. Under it, the chosen run as a table — on a desktop window the sheet spans the content width for it rather than taking the detail half — — subject (with who · when under it) · destination (the last three path components, the full path on hover) · files (each `.msg` a chip that opens through the [per-PC opener](folders.md#folders-that-open-on-any-pc); the first one shown, the rest behind a *+N more* chip that opens them in place) · confidence · why · state — and behind each row's **⋮** exactly the review levels that row's state allows (a row carries its file chip, *+N more* and the ⋮, nothing else): **Accept** (a mail that needed a human), **Move to… / File it…** (the candidates the run already ranked, or any other folder through the same index picker the drawer uses, with an optional one-line hint the next run remembers), **Retry** (on the one row whose file was written before Outlook refused the move: one tap re-sends the same decision and the mail leaves the Inbox) and **Revert** (the files are deleted and the mail goes back to the Inbox). A run picker lists earlier runs; *Accept all N that need you* clears a reviewed batch in one go; every action that touches a file asks first. The phone gets the same report as cards with the actions behind each row's *Review* — and the whole pane is drawn for 390 px: the head is one line with the three readings one line each under it (bold lead word, muted tail, the whole reading in the title), the run picker reaches the right edge with the bulk button full width under it as *Accept all (N)*, every review level is a plain glyph-only icon-button (`.archive-action`, its word the accessible name and tooltip — nothing outlined or filled at rest) with **Revert** alone behind the card's ⋮, the *File it…* panel puts the folder field's glyph inside its own right edge over a full-width *Archive here*, and nothing — a long subject, a deep path in a reason — can make the pane scroll sideways. The command palette's *Go to Archive* says **N mail(s) from the last run need you** when the last run left something undecided. See [Archive the Inbox](inbox-archive.md#archive-the-inbox).
- **Search** — one box over four indexes (tasks · folders · emails · issues), results as one card per kind, in the centred 772px column; a task hit is the standard row; a folder, email or issue hit is a title and one meta line, with one control that opens it. `Ctrl+K` / `⌘K` opens the **command palette** from any tab: type to jump to a task, `>` for commands. See [Search everything](search.md#search-everything).
- **Text size** — Settings' *Text size* row, changed in place: Small / Default / Large (the vendored `text-size` control as a vendored `segmented` group, from project-scaffolding), the escape from the viewport zoom lock for anyone who needs bigger text. It scales the root font-size (93.75% / 100% / 112.5%), so every rem-based type role grows with it while rows, buttons, the nav and icons keep their pixel geometry. The step is stored per device in `localStorage` (`task-os.textsize`) and stamped on `<html data-textsize>` by the inline pre-paint script in `index.html` — the same script that stamps the theme — so a reload paints at the chosen size with no flash.
- **Your name** — Settings → *Your name* (its sheet): a text field for the author recorded on the comments and activity this install writes. *Save name* writes `your_name` into the config file and applies it at once; blank clears it, and the row then reads the fallback (the first team member, else `me`). The row shows the saved name, or *not set*; the field's placeholder and the line under it show the name an unnamed write is signed with.
- **Full screen on Edge** — Settings' switch row, shown only in Edge (desktop `Edg/` or Android `EdgA/`) where the Fullscreen API exists, off by default (the vendored `fullscreen` component, from project-scaffolding). It exists because a managed phone's work-profile Edge cannot install the app, so it runs under Edge's address bar and toolbar. The switch's own tap enters full screen; the choice is stored per device in `localStorage` (`task-os.fullscreen`), so after a reload the first tap anywhere enters again, since a browser grants full screen to a gesture only. Leaving by the back gesture or Esc does not turn the setting off; the switch does.
- **Row actions** — Settings → *Row actions* (its sheet): what a **swipe right** and a **swipe left** on a task row run (any row action, or nothing; the plan's defaults are *complete* and *change date*), and which row actions the **⋯ menu** lists and in what order (tick, ↑ / ↓; *Reset to the defaults*). The choices are ids from the one action table (`actions.js`), so the swipes, the menu, the keys and the palette stay one source. An action a swipe runs is always kept in the menu, and every action keeps its key and palette entry, so none is ever gesture-only (WCAG 2.5.1). Stored per device in `localStorage` (`task-os.rowactions`, `rowprefs.js`), like the text size.
- **Quick-add** — the `+` in every pane's top strip (Board / Today) opens one dialog: line 1 is natural language, parsed server-side by `POST /api/parse` (so the CLI and the UI agree) — the dates it finds land in the **Due** and **Starts** fields where you can still correct them, the parent shows as a chip. Under it sit the fields worth setting while the task is still in your head, all optional and empty by default (no date, status `todo`): **project** (a select of the open projects; it opens on the project the list is filtered to, and a parent typed on line 1 wins over it) and **owner** (the members, opening on the person this device is; no control for a one-member install) first, then **description**, **due**, **starts**, **status**, **folder** (typed as a ref / an absolute path, or picked from the folder index — the same picker the drawer uses) and **one link** (url + label; its kind is classified the same way the drawer classifies a pasted link). `type · Enter` is still the whole fast path. Escape, the backdrop and the × discard the draft; Escape closes the folder picker first when it is open:

  | You type | Result |
  | --- | --- |
  | `renew passport next friday` | title `renew passport`, due = next Friday |
  | `pay water bill by tomorrow` · `… on fri` · `… in 2 weeks` | trailing date phrase (optional `on` / `by` / `due` lead-in) — every phrase `src/dates.py` knows |
  | `renew insurance due oct 15 starts oct 1` | both dates off one line — `starts` needs its keyword, a bare trailing date is the due date ([Start dates](task-workflow.md#start-dates)) |
  | `order sensor #12` | nested under task 12 |
  | `order sensor › garden-bot` (or `> garden-bot`) | nested under the open task whose title matches (exact title wins, then a task that already has children) |
  | `fix tap › Bathroom tomorrow` | parent **and** date |

  A parent that matches nothing shows as a red chip and nothing is created. Enter creates the task and focuses its row.
